# The footer parsers of the collectors

The rules that the footer readers of the two collectors follow, `FooterReader`, `ParquetFooter.Parse`, and the parser behind them in C#, and `FooterReader` and `parse_footer` of `pqax.collect` in Python, and how both make the physical columns of a table from the leaves of a schema. They were written down on 28 October 2026 for the run against the files of other writers and against mutated footers, which [README.md](README.md#footers-of-other-writers) describes, and the collector tests of both languages name them. "The footer error" is `ParquetFooterException` in C# and `FooterError` in Python, with the issue code in `Code` or `code`. The choices that the documents of the format leave open are proposals in [decisions.md](../docs/decisions.md).

## The outcome of a read

- **R1.** A read returns a footer or raises the footer error with `FooterInvalid`, `FooterEncrypted`, or `FooterTooLarge`, for any bytes and any stated size. On bad bytes nothing else escapes: no stack overflow, no `RecursionError`, no `MemoryError` or `OutOfMemoryException`, no `IndexOutOfRangeException`, `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`, or `OverflowException`, no `ValueError`, `UnicodeError`, or `struct.error`. A failed read of the storage raises the error of the storage, as before. `ParquetFooter.Parse` and `parse_footer`, on the bytes of a footer alone, follow the same rule.
- **R2.** The same bytes and the same stated size give the same outcome in both languages: the same code and message, or footers with the same value in every member of the dump of `footer_check.py`.
- **R3.** The checks come in this order: a stated size below 12; a first read shorter than asked, "The file is shorter than the size that the table format gives."; the last 4 bytes, where `PARE` is `FooterEncrypted` and anything but `PAR1` is `FooterInvalid`; the length before the magic, little endian and signed, which is `FooterInvalid` when it is not above 0 or when it is above the size less 12, and `FooterTooLarge` above 64 MiB; a second read that is short, `FooterInvalid`; then the parse.
- **R4.** A read takes time linear in the length of the footer and memory within a fixed multiple of it, and never hangs. No count read from the footer allocates more than the bytes left can hold.
- **R5.** A list or a map of the footer holds at most 1,000,000 elements, the limit of a Thrift container in the Parquet reader of Arrow C++ (`FooterReader.MaxListLength`, `MAX_LIST_LENGTH`), and the row groups of a footer hold at most 4,194,304 column chunks, and as many sorting columns, in all, every list read counting (`FooterReader.MaxColumnChunks`, `MAX_COLUMN_CHUNKS`). Past either, the read is `FooterTooLarge`: "A list of the footer has N elements, more than the limit of 1000000.", "A map of the footer has N entries, more than the limit of 1000000.", "The row groups of the footer hold more column chunks than the limit of 4194304.", or the same with sorting columns. The check comes right after the header of the list, after the check that its elements fit the bytes left, which stays `FooterInvalid`.
- **R6.** A field header whose type is 0, the bytes 0x10 to 0xF0, ends its structure whatever its delta, as the Thrift libraries of C++, Java, and Rust read it.

## The parse

- **P1.** Every structure, list, set, and map counts one level, whether the parser reads it or skips it: the footer is level 1, `row_groups` level 2, a row group 3, its `columns` 4, a column chunk 5, its `meta_data` 6, and its `statistics` 7. More than 64 levels is `FooterInvalid`, "The footer nests values more than 64 levels deep."
- **P2.** The schema tree has at most 256 levels below its root, "The schema of the footer is nested too deep." otherwise, and the columns of a folder of Parquet files get their type for every schema that the parser reads.
- **P3.** A field that appears twice takes its last value, and a structure that appears twice, such as `meta_data` or `statistics`, replaces the first whole; a second `columns` list of a row group replaces the first. A field of an unknown id, and a known field of an unexpected wire type, is skipped. An empty list may give any type to its elements, as fastparquet writes the row groups of a file without rows. A footer that lacks `version`, `schema`, `num_rows`, or `row_groups`, a schema element without `name`, a row group without `columns`, `num_rows`, or `total_byte_size`, a `meta_data` without `num_values`, a size, or `data_page_offset`, and a row group whose column chunks differ in number from the leaves, are `FooterInvalid`.
- **P4.** Every string is read as UTF-8, with U+FFFD for each maximal invalid subpart: the decoders of .NET and Python agree. The keys of the allow-list (`vorder`, `org.apache.spark.version`, `string_prefix_length`), the keys and the value of V-Order (`vorder.enabled` = `true`, `vorder.level`), and the names of the columns that the leaves map to are compared as .NET `OrdinalIgnoreCase` compares them: by the upper case of each character, when that is one character, where a character outside ASCII never folds into ASCII. So `ſ` is not `s`, `ı` is not `i`, and the Kelvin sign is not `k`, while `é` is `É`.
- **P5.** With data values, a `DECIMAL` bound has text only when its unscaled value takes at most 32 bytes and its scale is at most 76; otherwise it is null.

## Nested columns

- **N1.** Each leaf of the schema is one physical column, in the order of the leaves. Its `Path` holds every name from the root, the root left out, to the leaf, with the names of the levels of lists and maps as the file has them: `list` and `element`, `key_value`, `key`, and `value`, or the older `array`, `item`, `bag`, and `<name>_tuple`.
- **N2.** `PhysicalType`, `TypeLength`, `Repetition`, `FieldId`, and `LogicalType`, the annotation, are those of the leaf itself, not of the groups above it.
- **N3.** The `Column` of a leaf is its top-level column: by the field id of its top-level field when the file and the table have field ids, and otherwise by the first name of its path, compared with the physical name of a Delta column under column mapping, or with its name, exactly and then without case when one column only matches. A leaf whose top-level field the table does not declare has no `Column`, and counts in `StaleColumnSize`.
- **N4.** The totals of a column are over the column chunks of all its leaves: `CompressedSize`, `UncompressedSize`, `ChunkCount`, `ValueCount`, the sum of `num_values`, `NullCount`, `DictionaryChunkCount`, `FallbackChunkCount`, `DictionaryPageSize`, `UnencodedByteArrayDataBytes` over the leaves of `BYTE_ARRAY`, `Codecs`, `Encodings`, and `LargestChunkDistinctCount`, the largest over all the leaves. `FileCountWithoutColumn` counts the files whose schema has no leaf of the column.
- **N5.** In a folder of Parquet files, the `DataType` of a nested column is in the names of the Delta schema, as Spark reads the file: `array<t>`, `map<k,v>`, and `struct<a:t,b:u>`, with the rules of Spark for the lists of two levels and the legacy maps, and `IsNullable` comes from the top-level field.
- **N6.** Both collectors give the same physical schemas, physical columns, columns, and totals for the files of every writer.

## The samples

`samples/footers` holds the Parquet files of the kit with `expected.json`, as [README.md](README.md#the-footer-samples) describes: the files that the parsers once read wrong, and the nested columns of six writers. Both collector suites read every member, and `cross_check.py` checks the two parsers and the two collectors on them.
